The koi is drawn by git-koi from my public activity, and its pond changes with the seasons.

How the pond is decided

  • The fish itself comes from git-koi, which reads your public GitHub activity every night: your languages colour its markings, your total contributions make it bigger, streaks add markings, and how much you did the day before decides how it swims.
  • The body colour is a choice, not data. With body: auto each scene gets the colour that suits it: asagi in spring, gold in summer and rain, tea in autumn, black in winter, platinum at night. Or pick one fish and keep it all year.
  • The season comes from the date when the job runs, just after midnight UTC: March to May spring, June to August summer, September to November autumn, December to February winter. Outside summer, about one day in seven is a rain day instead; which days is fixed by the date, so a rerun on the same day draws the same pond.
  • Night is not the time of day. A README cannot know the viewer's clock, but it can know their theme: the job draws a second GIF, and GitHub shows it to anyone in dark mode.
  • Leaves and petals: with the fixed pattern, each one lands in the same spot on every loop. Randomised, each lands somewhere new each time; in the GIF that variety lives inside one loop, and the seed is the date, so tomorrow's pond drops them differently.

What the fork changes from git-koi

  • Untouched: how the koi is drawn. The fork downloads git-koi's own released program, checks its checksum, and draws the fish exactly as the original does.
  • Added to the Action: a scene input (none, auto, or a named scene), a night input that also draws the dark-mode GIF, and body: auto. All of them default to off, so the fork behaves like the original unless you switch them on.
  • Added code: scenes/pond.py, which paints the pond and swims the finished koi through it, and a short section in the README. The pond edges, lamps and moons on this page are new options for that file and are not in the fork yet: they go in once you pick.
